Mój Dzionek

A preschool’s day in parents’ pockets: the daily report, messages and a group chat, notes from the teachers, absences, pickups, a calendar, the menu and the month’s payments, with a web panel for the director. Made for Polish preschools and the families they serve.

React Native Expo Kotlin Ktor

Overview

Mój Dzionek (Polish for “my little day”) connects a preschool with the families it looks after. Teachers record the day as it happens — who arrived, what they ate, how long they slept — and parents see it on their phones the same afternoon, along with messages, the group chat, the calendar and the menu.

It is built around how a preschool group actually works: one shared tablet per group with a personal PIN for each teacher, entries made for the whole group in one tap, and the day-to-day logistics of absences, meals and pickups handled in the same place. The director runs the preschool from a web panel. Parents can use the app in Polish, Ukrainian or English. The screenshots show the demo preschool in Polish.

For parents

  • The child’s day on the phone: arrival and departure, meals, nap, mood, activities and the teacher’s notes, with an evening notification that the summary is ready
  • Absences reported in the app; one reported before the morning cut-off cancels that day’s meals too
  • Who is collecting the child today, picked from the people the parent has authorised
  • A month and week calendar with attendance colours, events and holidays, which can also be added to Google or Apple Calendar
  • Private messages with the group’s teachers, a chat for the whole group, and announcements
  • Notes from the teachers about the child — praise, a remark or an incident — with a notification for each
  • The month’s payment with how it adds up (fee, meals, meals refunded for absences), where to pay, and an “I paid” button
  • The weekly menu, the app in Polish, Ukrainian or English, and a choice of which notifications to get

For teachers

  • One entry for the whole group at once — arrival, meal, nap, mood, activity or note — then just the exceptions
  • Recently written notes come back as one-tap suggestions, and naps start at the usual time
  • A shared group tablet with a personal PIN for each teacher, so every entry is signed and private phones stay private
  • Pickup at the door: authorised people with their ID, today’s plan, and court restrictions enforced by the server
  • Allergies next to the menu, the kitchen’s meal count after absences, and last weeks’ menus to copy
  • Notes about a child for its parents — praise, a remark or an incident — showing which parents have read them
  • No notifications outside the preschool’s working hours

For the director

  • A web panel that opens on today: attendance per group, absences with reasons and planned pickups
  • Announcements to the whole preschool or one group, with read counts
  • Children pasted in from a spreadsheet, groups, the team and their roles, and a first-steps checklist for a new preschool
  • Invite codes to print, each with a QR code for parents to scan
  • The month’s payments in one place instead of on paper: the fee and meals worked out per child, with last month’s cancelled meals taken off, any amount adjusted, and payments marked as they come in. Nothing is processed.
  • The monthly attendance and meals sheet as CSV, and figures on how the preschool uses the app
  • An audit log of every change, opening hours and the meal cut-off, and the subscription with invoice details

Privacy by design

  • Everyone sees only their own: a parent their child, a teacher their groups, the director the whole preschool
  • Health details only with a parent’s consent, gone when it is withdrawn; nothing personal in notifications
  • Data export and account deletion in the app; a child’s data removed 90 days after leaving, and someone left in no preschool anonymised
